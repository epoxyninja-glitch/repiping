The material

What is polybutylene?

Polybutylene is a plastic water-supply piping material used in some homes built from the late 1970s through the mid-1990s. It is distinct from drain lines. Its history of premature failures is why it is often flagged during inspections and insurance reviews.

A construction date alone does not confirm the material currently installed. Partial replacements are common. Accessible piping must be evaluated before any recommendation is made.

Questions

Short answers.

How do I know whether my home has polybutylene?

An address or construction year does not establish the material currently installed. Accessible piping must be evaluated. Renovations and partial replacements are common.

Can you identify it from a photograph?

A clear image of accessible piping or printed markings may help with preliminary identification. It does not document the entire concealed system. Do not cut pipes or open walls to take a photograph.

What if some pipes have already been replaced?

Tell us what was replaced and where. The assessment considers prior work when preparing any scope.

Does repiping include drains?

The proposal must say. Water-supply replacement and drain-line work are not interchangeable scopes.

What happens to the walls and ceilings?

The written proposal identifies who handles patching, texture, painting, and any other restoration — and what is excluded.

How is the price determined?

By the actual piping, property layout, access, material, and restoration scope. The proposal explains what is included before work is authorized.

What documentation is available afterward?

Completion documentation is described in the proposal. Specific requirements should be confirmed with the contractor.

Will the work satisfy my insurer?

Ask your insurer or agent for its specific requirements. The contractor can describe the proposed work and available documentation but should not promise an underwriting decision.
